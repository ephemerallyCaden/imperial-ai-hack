## Nardini and Gevertz, arXiv 2606.10873 (CC BY 4.0), "Spatial Model Selection and UQ: Continuous vs Discrete Wound Healing Models"
- Four lattice ABMs vs four mean-field PDEs fitted by ABC (10^4 uniform prior samples, keep MSE <= 1.25 x min, 90% HPD intervals). Data: Jin 2016 PC-3, 6 densities, 5 times (0 to 2 days), 37 bins, 3 replicates averaged. ABM: 74x60 lattice, 25 um, Gillespie, pulling and logistic delay; ranges rm [0,0.25], p_pull [0,1], rp [0,5], a0 [-20,20], a1 [0,40].
- Results: PDEs and ABMs recover parameters comparably (ABM intervals wider); PDE wins AIC on 6 of 8 synthetic datasets; on real data PDE model 4 wins AIC at 5 of 6 densities; PDE 0.028 s vs ABM 37 s per simulation.
- Forecast: fit times 1 to 4, score MSE at time 5 only; parameter intervals are not propagated; no coverage, no baselines.
- Corrections: AIC 720 vs 842.4 are different prior-sample sizes (10^6 vs 10^4), not an inconsistency; migration and proliferation rates (rm, rp) were fairly well identified; pulling and delay were not. Code: github.com/johnnardini/Spatial_model_selection_UQ (licence not stated).

## Lagergren et al. 2020, PLoS CB (BINNs, CC BY), doi 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008462
- Same Jin data. Neural network with learned diffusion D(u), growth G(u) and delay T(t); showed a delay term fixes high-density discrepancy; guided model beats FKPP and Porous-FKPP on 5 of 6 densities (AIC). No uncertainty quantification, no held-out-time test, in-sample AIC. Protocol: 20 restarts, Adam, early stop 5000 epochs.

## Nardini et al. 2021, arXiv 2011.08255 (equation learning from stochastic ABMs)
- Learns ODEs from averaged ABM runs; 10 to 25 runs suffice; learned model beats mean-field as clustering grows. Early-data limit: with 10% of the curve the fit diverges, 20% plateaus wrongly, 25% test MSE 0.0021, 50% 0.0002; needs data past the inflection point. Learned equations do not extrapolate; no spread/calibration.

## MNCA, Milite, Caravagna, Sottoriva, arXiv 2506.20486 (CC BY 4.0)
- Each cell draws one of K=5 rules (Gumbel-Softmax); optional noise; 35x35 grid, 6 channels, hidden 128, Adam 1e-3, 800 epochs, 200 simulated tissues, MSE loss. Synthetic only (own simulator, emoji, synthetic BBBC031); the Visium claim in the text is unsupported. Table 2: KL 0.018 (MNCA) vs 2.057 (NCA); ABC baseline has better Border-W (0.054 vs 0.184). No official code link; no calibration or conservation test.

## NeuralCPM, Minartz et al., arXiv 2502.02129
- Cellular Potts with learned (neural) Hamiltonian, not an NCA; persistent contrastive divergence; real data = six final aggregates plus one shared time-lapse (Toda 2018), validation only; up to about 100 cells; no division; assumes equilibrium. No code link in the text.

## Richardson et al. 2024, PLoS CB 20(4):e1011589 (CC BY)
- NCA with fixed 3x3 kernels, hidden channels, stochastic update mask; Euclidean loss best; synthetic only (Gray-Scott, emoji); extrapolates 2x in time; rules non-unique; regeneration and real data are future work. Code: github.com/AlexDR1998/NCA (TensorFlow; licence not in text).

## Portela, Banga, Matabuena 2025, PLoS CB 21(5):e1013098 (CC BY), conformal UQ for dynamic models
- Jackknife+ based CUQDyn1/2; intervals for observed variables at observed times only; coverage stated qualitatively; split conformal inefficient for n<20. Data/code on Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.13644869 (licence of code not seen). Not a forecasting guarantee.

## Jin, Shah, Penington, McCue, Chopin, Simpson 2016, J Theor Biol 390:136 (PDF read in full via the user's Imperial download; doi 10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.10.040)
- Experiment: PC-3 prostate cancer cells; IncuCyte ZOOM, 96-well ImageLock plates, WoundMaker scratch repeated 20 times, two washes, 100 uL fresh medium. Seeded at 10,000, 12,000, 14,000, 16,000, 18,000, 20,000 cells per well, grown overnight before scratching. Images every 2 hours for 48 hours; n=3 identically prepared replicates per density.
- Data extraction: each image cut into 39 columns of 50 um (38 used), cells counted manually per column (positions x = 25, 75, ..., 1925 um), mean and SD over replicates at t = 0, 12, 24, 36, 48 h only (so the 5-time-point limit comes from manual counting, not imaging). Carrying capacity K = 1.7e-3 cells/um^2. Mean and SD profiles are in Supplementary Material Documents 2 to 7 (6 files); the PDF states no licence for them (Elsevier). No raw images are provided in the supplement.
- Fisher-Kolmogorov fits (Table 1, least squares over a D-lambda error surface; checked with MATLAB Levenberg-Marquardt): D (um^2/h) about 310, 250, 720, 570, 760, 1030 for 10K to 20K (with intervals of tens to hundreds); lambda (/h) 0.044, 0.044, 0.048, 0.049, 0.054, 0.064; doubling time 15.75 to 10.83 h. So D varies about 310% and lambda about 45% across starting densities; the authors conclude FK (constant D, lambda) does not describe the process. Reading of garbled table digits: verify against the PDF before quoting.
- Porous-Fisher fits (Table 2): D about 1300 to 3000 um^2/h (wide intervals), lambda the same as FK, so D varies far less; the match to data is judged excellent; the authors say Porous-Fisher describes the experiments better (D = D0 (C/K)^m form).
- Takeaways for us: (1) confirms density dependence of effective spreading, so the cell simulator needs density-dependent movement; (2) lambda rises with density in the fits, which is itself puzzling and worth showing as a drift; (3) the 5 manual-count profiles per density, 3 replicates (SD available) give the replicate variance that a calibration test needs, but only 6 densities; (4) doubling times of 11 to 16 h set the proliferation scale for the simulator.
- Open: Elsevier terms for reusing the supplement data; do not re-host files, cite the paper.

## Cai, Li, Shi, Cai, Zhou 2025, Regenerative Therapy (PMC12528918, CC BY-NC-ND): "Modeling epithelial wound closure dynamics with AI: A comparative study across cell types"
- Uses WHAD (MCF7 and MCF10A). Segmentation: UNet++ with attention and multi-scale input vs Otsu (80:20 train-validation split). Closure percent = (A0 - At)/A0 per frame.
- Temporal modelling is on the frame-wise MEAN closure curve of each cell type, with frame ID as input: polynomial, random forest, SVR, ARIMA(1,1,1), TCN. Split is 80/20 over time points of the mean curve. Reported: RF MAE 0.48% and R2 0.968 (MCF7), MAE 1.73% and R2 0.872 (MCF10A); TCN MCF7 MAE 1.67%, R2 0.698; ARIMA test R2 negative (-0.227, -0.298) with MAE 4.06% and 13.52%.
- Reading: this is curve-fitting of the group-average trajectory, not per-wound early forecasting; no held-out wounds, no uncertainty or calibration, no knockdown-vs-control test. The authors list as limitations the small number of temporal frames and lack of stage labels. A useful published comparator and the likely scooping risk for "AI closure forecasting on WHAD", but our per-wound, calibrated, early-call, held-out-condition framing is not covered. Note licence is NC-ND: cite, do not reproduce.
- Also found: LILAC (Kim et al., PNAS 2025, PMC11873959, CC BY): Siamese network predicting temporal ordering/time difference of longitudinal images, applied to wound healing among other tasks (r = 0.875 with time interval); not a forecaster.

## Iheme et al. 2024, IEEE Data Descriptions vol. 1, doi 10.1109/ieeedata.2024.3481394 (open access under a Creative Commons licence; read via the Imperial network, no login)
- WHAD: 11 experiments, 5,490 frames; time-lapse phase-contrast of MCF10A and MCF7 wound healing assays. Seeded in 12-well plates (1e6 cells/well MCF10A, 7.5e5 MCF7); 2-hour 10 ug/mL MITOMYCIN C treatment to BLOCK PROLIFERATION; images every HOUR for 48 or 72 h (Leica DMI8). Pixel size 0.454 um (10x objective), images 1920x1440 (field about 870 x 650 um), so individual cells are resolvable.
- Conditions (Table I): MCF10A: control, NICD overexpression, CYR61 shRNA, NICD + CYR61 shRNA (file prefixes such as C61, N61, CC); MCF7: control, SEMA6D, LacZ overexpression, SEMA6D overexpression (SEMA6D overexpression is reported to increase MCF7 migration). Per-experiment details are in the supplement whad_experiments_summary.xlsx (not read).
- Annotation: manual polygons of open wound regions in ImageJ and Supervisely by two annotators, approved by a biologist; masks for the main cell group, detached cell groups and empty regions; the number of annotated frames per experiment varies with healing speed (this explains the 19 to 25 frame series and partial mask coverage).
- Implications: WHAD wounds close by migration only (no division), so it cannot validate a proliferation readout, but it is a clean test of the migration part and of density-dependent movement; cells are resolvable, so a cell-scale view can be sanity-checked against real images (no tracks provided).

## Migliaccio, Ferraro, Wang, Cristini, Dogra, Caserta 2023, Cancers 15(21):5284 (CC BY; PMID 37958456; PDF read in full from the user's download): "Exploring Cell Migration Mechanisms in Cancer: From Wound Healing Assays to Cellular Automata Models"
- A research article (not a review). A cellular-automaton "digital twin" of the in vitro wound healing (scratch) assay, calibrated and validated on in vitro data from 4 cell lines (HT-1080, MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, HaCaT) and then 5 more (MCF-7, NIH/3T3, Saos-2, Caco-2, BEAS), plus one new experiment (Table 2: 21 experiment IDs with density, closure rate alpha, wound width b0, doubling time Td, migration time Tm).
- Model: 2D square lattice, one cell per site (site size delta = average cell size); left and right cell domains separated by a cell-free wound of width b0; periodic/refilled boundary columns to keep density constant. Each step each cell either migrates (probability Pm = (1/Tm)/(1/Tm + 1/Td)) or proliferates (Pd = (1/Td)/(1/Tm + 1/Td)), with Tm = delta^2 / D (D = random motility coefficient) and Td = doubling time. Cells with no empty neighbour are quiescent (contact inhibition). Migration is biased toward the wound by reducing available neighbour sites from 9 to 7 on the far side. Isotropic otherwise.
- Output used for validation: wound area A/A0 over time; closure rate alpha = slope of the linear decrease of A/A0. Reported Pearson correlation of about 0.98 between simulated and measured curves (calibration-quality agreement, not a held-out forecast). Local and global sensitivity analyses over (Tm, Td, rho, delta, b0).
- Main result: from 2000 simulations, closure rate follows a power law alpha = a * Phi^(-b) in the dimensionless number Phi = Tm / Td (reported a about 2e-3, b about 0.66 from the extracted text; verify in the PDF), spanning Phi from 1e-4 to 0.5; the fit under-predicts by about 25% at Phi > 0.1. A critical Phi below about 1e-2 means proliferation can be neglected (closure is motility-driven), which the authors say can simplify experimental protocols.
- Limits stated: 2D only; MATLAB; data available on request only (no public code or data); 3D, matrix stiffness, chemotaxis left to future work.
- Relevance to Stitch: (1) a published, validated lattice agent model whose rules (migrate or proliferate, quiescence = contact inhibition, wound-directed bias) can be reimplemented as the cell-agent layer; (2) Phi = Tm/Td and the power law give a cheap closed-form baseline for closure rate and an a-priori check on whether division matters (division off is justified for mitomycin-treated WHAD when Phi is small); (3) Table 2 gives doubling times and migration times for many cell lines (HaCaT, MDA-MB-231, MCF-7 and others) as priors for presets; (4) caution: validation is on fitted closure rates, not held-out forecasts or uncertainty, so our calibration and held-out evaluation remain a gap; the model uses mutually exclusive migrate/proliferate per step, a different way to combine movement and multiplication than separate flux and source terms.

## Chadwick 2025, Wounds International (13 Oct 2025): "The role of artificial intelligence in wound care: applications, evidence and future directions" (narrative review; page read via Firefox)
- Overview of ML, deep learning and NLP in wound care: computer vision for wound imaging, predictive analytics for healing trajectories, smart dressings, clinical decision support. No new datasets or methods.
- Useful pointers: Berezo et al. 2022, "Predicting Chronic Wound Healing Time Using Machine Learning" (Advances in Wound Care; existence verified; the review describes decision-tree models on about 1.2 million electronic health records predicting 12-week healing from area-reduction rate, depth, comorbidities; not read, so verify before citing); skin-tone bias in AI wound imaging is listed as an open challenge; regulation (FDA pre-market approval, CE mark, ISO 13485, GDPR/HIPAA) supports our "research prototype, not a medical device" framing.
- Use: related-work and framing only; cite as a review.

## Berezo, Budman, Deutscher, Hess, Smith, Hayes 2022, Advances in Wound Care (PMC8982125, CC BY; doi 10.1089/wound.2021.0073): "Predicting Chronic Wound Healing Time Using Machine Learning" (read in full via Europe PMC)
- Gradient-boosted decision trees on EHR data to predict wounds NOT healed within 4, 8 and 12 weeks of starting treatment; three data sets totalling 1,220,576 wounds; 187 covariates (demographics, comorbidities, wound characteristics); AUC 0.854, 0.855, 0.853 for the 4-, 8- and 12-week models. Most influential (SHAP): days in treatment, wound depth and location, wound area.
- Data: Net Health Wound Care Analysis Data Set (proprietary, not available). Split: patient-level, 60% train / 15% validation / 25% test. Healed = final status Resolved/Healed/Graft/Closed/Treatment Complete before the timeframe; non-healed includes Amputated and Quit Treatment.
- Stated limitations: retrospective; no prospective validation; no treatments/interventions as covariates; cannot capture recurring wounds; needs validation outside the Net Health data. No uncertainty quantification or calibration reported in the abstract.
- Relevance: the clinical, EHR-scale baseline for "will a wound heal in N weeks"; we differ (in vitro or simulated closure curves, per-wound forecast with calibrated bands, no patient covariates). It also shows wound area and time in treatment carry most signal, consistent with using area trajectories.

## Dallmann, Sheridan, Mattke, Ennis 2025, Advances in Wound Care (doi 10.1089/wound.2024.0095; abstract only): "Prediction of Healing Trajectory of Chronic Wounds Using a Machine Learning Approach"
- EHR data from 532 US wound clinics, 261,398 patients, 620,356 wounds (2014 to 2018); predicts healing by 12 weeks; AUC 0.90 after 4 weeks and 0.92 after 5 weeks of treatment; changes in wound dimensions over time were the most influential predictors. Not read in full; open-access status not confirmed. Closest published "early call from the area trajectory" result in clinical data; cite and compare conceptually.
