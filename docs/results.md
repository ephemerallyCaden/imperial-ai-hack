## What does not work (said plainly)

- **Calibration does not transfer to a faster-closing cell type.** Bands calibrated on ordinary MCF-7 wells cover only 15-58% of the stem-cell wells' future (target 90%); 97% of their observations fall below the forecast median.
- **Held-out condition on WHAD is tighter than nominal early on**: CC to C61 coverage is lowest at the earliest cut-off and recovers with more history.
- **No 'measure again' signal**: band width, history-fit error and stall ambiguity give AUC 0.61-0.72 for predicting a bad forecast, inconsistent across cut-offs.
- **The stall flag is a prompt to look, not a verdict**: when it fires it is right only 48%-75% of the time across the checks (it ranks stalls well, AUC above 0.77, but stalls are rare). The agent replay agrees: about half of all stall stops are wrong (agent/results/scoreboard.md). That is why a fired flag means 'ask a human', never an automatic stop.
- **Simple models are strong**: a 2-parameter constant-edge-speed model is the best forecaster on WHAD; more history helps every model.
- **On homogeneous sets the typical-wound curve wins early**: on WHiM-BC, fly embryo and Zaritsky, the population-average curve beats extrapolating the wound's own rate at ~20% of history. Per-wound rates only pay off where wounds differ a lot (WHAD).
- **Small numbers**: 28 WHAD wounds (9 with manual masks); coverage CIs are about +/-0.05 to +/-0.15.
- **Time-to-90% is often censored**: most WHAD series do not reach 90% closure within 19 h, so t90 medians are often open-ended.

## Can a scientist stop the plate early? (simulation)

Policy fixed in advance: at each check (1.9, 3.8, 5.7, 9.5 h), if P(90% closed by 19 h) <= 0.1 or >= 0.9, make the call and stop imaging; otherwise keep imaging. Held-out WHAD wounds, edge-speed forecaster. This is a simulation on recorded data, not a lab trial.

- **45 of 55 series (82%) get a call by the first check** (median 1.9 h); 91% of calls are right (4 wrong), vs 75% for always saying 'won't close'.
- Every early call is **'won't reach 90% closure by 19 h'** (45 calls); the forecaster never confidently calls early closure. The wrong calls are wounds that did close: stopping them would have been a mistake.
- Simulated imaging saved: 13.9 h per series on average (of 19 h).
- Same policy with the shipped forecaster (real-data NCA v3): 45 of 55 series called by the first check, 91% of calls right (4 wrong).

**Gate decisions.** R18: the simulator-trained NCA was not passable at the headline cut-off (30% worse than edge speed at 3.8 h). **R26: fine-tuned on real WHAD frames (4 wound-level folds, final weights, no test-fold selection) the NCA passes 9 of 12 and every cut-off on unseen wounds: 3.8 h quantile score 0.0403 vs edge speed 0.0386 (+4%), and it is the best model of all at 9.5 h.** Adding Zaritsky wells made it worse (3/12). It still fails the held-out condition (CC to C61) at 1.9 h and 9.5 h (coverage 0.78-0.79). Raw NCA bands cover only 10-17% of the future; the conformal step is what makes them honest. Caveat: the calibration pool for a wound uses other folds' models, which may have trained on that wound (its own forecast never does).

## Held-out condition: what fixes the under-coverage? (v4, design.md R28)

The pre-registered test (calibrate on CC, test on C61) under-covers. Tested mitigations, reported next to it, not instead of it: **rest** = calibrate on all non-C61 conditions (CC + N61 + NC); **rest + inflate** = also widen the band by a factor measured on known conditions only (leave-one-condition-out among CC/N61/NC, largest factor, cap 3x; fixed before looking at C61). Coverage of the 90% band on C61 wounds (target 0.90) / quantile score:

| Model | Calibration | 1.9 h | 3.8 h | 5.7 h | 9.5 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| edge speed | CC only (pre-registered) | 0.78 / 0.0794 | 0.92 / 0.0345 | 0.86 / 0.0344 | 0.88 / 0.0318 |
| edge speed | rest | 0.78 / 0.0827 | 0.96 / 0.0379 | 0.87 / 0.0371 | 0.94 / 0.0373 |
| edge speed | rest + inflate | 0.84 / 0.0805 | 0.98 / 0.0384 | 0.89 / 0.0366 | 1.00 / 0.0472 |
| NCA real-data v3 (calibrated) | CC only (pre-registered) | 0.81 / 0.0722 | 0.88 / 0.0388 | 0.85 / 0.0360 | 0.72 / 0.0282 |

Inflation lifts the earliest cut-off towards nominal but over-widens later ones (coverage up to 1.00, worse quantile score): it is a safety fallback for a truly unknown condition, which is how the upload path uses it (`uncalibrated_condition` flag), not a free fix.

## Can closure curves separate movement from division? (Jin 2016 PC-3, Fisher-KPP)

Fisher-KPP (u_t = D u_xx + r u(1 - u/K), K = 1.7e-3 cells/um^2) fitted over a grid of (D, r) at each of 6 initial densities, once to the full density profiles and once to the closure curve derived from the same profiles. Ranges = 95% region (`eval/jin_identifiability.py`, figure `eval/figures/jin_identifiability.png`).

| Initial density | Profiles: D (um^2/h) | Profiles: r (1/h) | Profile fit R^2 | Closure only: D range | Closure only: r range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10000 | 315 (232-429) | 0.042 | 0.90 | x10 | x18 |
| 12000 | 271 (199-315) | 0.042 | 0.95 | x9 | x5 |
| 14000 | 792 (680-792) | 0.048 | 0.94 | x14 | x2 |
| 16000 | 583 (583-680) | 0.048 | 0.95 | x29 | x26 |
| 18000 | 792 (680-792) | 0.054 | 0.95 | x34 | x39 |
| 20000 | 1077 (1077-1256) | 0.062 | 0.94 | x63 | x39 |

**Closure curves alone do not separate cell movement from division** (D uncertain over 9-63x, r over 2-39x, often along a constant-front-speed valley); **full density profiles do** (D within ~20%, r within one grid step, R^2 0.90-0.95). Fitted D rises with initial density, as Jin et al. reported. So Stitch, which sees wound area only, does not claim to separate the two.
